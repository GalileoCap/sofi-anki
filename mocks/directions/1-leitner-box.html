Direction 1 of 3: the Leitner box

All three123

The bet: the schedule is a box you can see. Home is the box itself, its compartments the places the scheduler has put every card, from every deck, and studying is the front compartment, one tap away.

The one bold element is the box: five compartments (today, tomorrow, this week, later, new), each card drawn as an edge in its deck's colour, so the place and the deck read at a glance. It comes from the Leitner box, the wooden box that spaced repetition began as, where a card moves forward when known and back to the front when missed. The app's scheduler does the same (O3), so the picture is true.

What it changes: task 1 (study what is due, on a phone) is one tap from opening the app, across decks. Today it is four steps inside one deck. Decks drop to a list ranked by what is due, below the box. After a grade, the card's edge moves to its new compartment, which is the only motion besides the card turning.

References: the Leitner box, school notebook covers (each deck's colour as its mark), a library reading room for the quiet around it.
