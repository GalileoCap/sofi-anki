The bet: studying fills a sticker sheet. The daily goal is twenty spots, and each card studied puts down a sticker in its deck's colour, so the cute part is the progress itself.

The one bold element is the sticker sheet, from the reward charts of a classroom. It shows real data: today's twenty spots are the daily goal, and the week's row is the streak, a sticker for each day studied, in the colour of the deck studied that day. Decks are notebook covers in their own colour, the emoji they chose set on them as a sticker. Grades are round rubber stamps, the kind a teacher presses on homework.

What it changes: home opens on today's sheet and one big Study button, then the covers ranked by due. Studying fills a spot after each grade, which is the feedback the brief allows. Everything cute carries something the person did or chose: no mascots, no props, no confetti.

References: a classroom sticker chart, a teacher's rubber stamps, school notebook covers, Japanese school stationery for the rounded type.
